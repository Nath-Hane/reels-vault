## Description
What you’ll wish you knew sooner… 🔻

It’s easy to say you’re not flexible. I said the same. Since then, I fractured my spine twice - and now I’m more flexible than almost anyone around me.

It’s not because I have good genetics. It’s not because I know a stretch that you don’t. It’s simply because I know HOW to stretch, and it’s simpler than you think…

1️⃣ FREQUENCY: Flexibility isn’t just about how ‘stretchy’ your muscles are. It’s your nervous system ALLOWING your muscles to relax.

To gain flexibility, you need to reach your nervous system you are safe and comfortable in these ranges of motion, and the best way you can do this is stretching OFTEN, and CONSISTENTLY.

The two day rule is the best method for progress. It’s normal, and okay to miss a day. But never go two consecutive days without stretching - this is where you’ll lose adaptations, especially at higher levels.

2️⃣ DURATION: I don’t stretch for long. Stretching often is more important. But if you want to progress, you still need to be stretching for at least one minute per muscle group, ideally more.

This doesn’t mean you should hold a stretch for one minute though - stretching for longer than 30s at a time brings about diminishing returns.

Instead, stretch for ~30s, rest, and stretch again. This prevents microtears forming and allows you to go deeper and gain flexibility each set.

3️⃣ SPECIFICITY: If you want flexibility for a specific skill, just stretching the muscle group isn’t enough. Flexibility is specific, so your stretching needs to reflect this.

For backbends I don’t just go into the bridge. I straighten my legs and lean massively into my shoulders, stretching them and simulating the angle my joints are at at the bottom of a hollowback.

You need to build STRENGTH in the antagonists (the muscles that shorten so the stretched muscle can lengthen) - these will help pull you deeper into the stretch if strengthen them enough.
