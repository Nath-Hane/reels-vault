## Transcription audio
My academic lab challenge is to read a paper a day, so here are the most interesting papers that you should be reading if you want to learn about different subjects. For economics, course 1960, the problem of social cost. This argues that externalities are best resolved through property rights and negotiation, basically founding the field of law and economics. And it isn't one of the most cited economics papers ever. Also, Akeloff 1970, the market for lemons. This helped him win the Nobel Prize, launched the era of information economics, and basically shows that asymmetry of information destroys markets. For political theory, you want to be reading Rawls, 1958, Justice as Fairness. This introduces the concept of the veil of ignorance and basically lays the foundations for what becomes the most influential book in 20th century political theory. Huntington 1993, The Clash of Civilizations, is probably one of the most controversial political theory papers that has ever been written. And it basically argues that any conflict after the Cold War would be cultural rather than ideological. For philosophy, you want to be reading Getty in 1963 is justified true belief knowledge. This paper is just three pages long, but it completely dismantles the 2000 year old platonic definition of knowledge. Quine 1951, Two Dogmas of Empiricism. This basically reshaped a birthed analytical philosophy. It is super fascinating. For sociology, I absolutely have to recommend Bordeaux 1986, The Forms of Capital. In this paper, he distinguishes economic, social and cultural capital and provides probably the most widely used framework for how class reproduces itself. Granavetta 1973, The Strength of Weak Ties. This is one of the most cited social science papers ever and it basically shows that your weak acquaintances will transmit more knowledge and opportunities to you than your close friends. Bonus, if you're at all interested in law, then Warren and Brandeis 1890, The Right to Privacy is fascinating. It is one of the most cited law review articles in history and the foundation of all modern privacy law. Let me know if you want me to do a part two.
