## Description
If you're just starting out with plyometrics, or just getting back into sports:
then start with extensive plyometrics — low-intensity, rhythmic, short ground contact movements that teach your body to be elastic and reactive.

this builds:
• tendon + ankle strength
• coordination and rhythm
• movement quality and control
• the foundation to safely progress to more intense work

Progressing slowly allows your body to adapt to these plyometrics so that you can avoid injuries by spiking your workload too fast. 

these exercises can be done 1–2x/week for complete beginners
80–100 ground contacts per session
rest 48–72 hours between days
