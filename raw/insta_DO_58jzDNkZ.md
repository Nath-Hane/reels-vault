## Transcription audio
This Japanese animation is one of the most beautiful films I have ever seen. But don't be fooled, because it tackles some really heavy stuff. This is day three of exploring some of the strangest, most obscure, and downright traumatic animated films from the 1970s. And today's film is genuinely unlike anything else you will ever see. This is Aichi Yamamoto's Belladonna of Sadness. On her wedding day, Jean's life is shattered after a brutal assault at the hands of the ruling baron. Out of desperation, she makes a pact with this mysterious demonic spirit to seek revenge against her abuser. The film traces this dark, surreal path as Jean becomes both a healer and an outcast, wielding her newfound power in a world that cannot accept a woman's strength. But this film isn't just provocative for the sake of it. It uses this juxtaposition of provocation and beauty to ask why women have always been demonized for wanting freedom, for chasing their desires, and for being defiant. You see, this film was never made to come for you, despite its gorgeous visuals. It just sort of leaves you there, staring at these images that are so beautiful. But yet, they're so hard to look at.
