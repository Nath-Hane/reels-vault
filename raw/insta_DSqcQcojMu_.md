## Transcription audio
I'm honestly not shitting you when I say that this is the best animated Christmas movie ever made, and most people have never heard of it. This is the final day of my 12 days of Christmas, where every day over the past 12 days, I've been sharing some of my favorite alternative picks for your annual festive viewing. And look, I know I talked about this movie in last year's series, but I will literally take any given opportunity to talk about it, so I'm doing it again, alright? This is Satoshi Khan's Tokyo Godfathers. The film starts with three homeless strangers, who are barely scraping by on the freezing streets of Tokyo. But when they stumble across an abandoned baby in a pile of trash, everything changes. And instead of doing the sensible thing and handing her over to the authorities, they decide to track down her parents themselves. And the search for the baby's parents sets off this chain reaction, dragging each of them back towards the lives they ran from. And the wounds they keep pretending don't hurt. But what I love, and what actually really kind of hurts about this film, is how it makes found family and redemption almost feel like the same thing. It doesn't do the whole they become a family, and then they heal thing. It's more so that the act of becoming a family, is the healing. And the whole arc of redemption doesn't just come in some big confessional moment of cleansing. It's actually a lot smaller and more vulnerable than that. It's choosing to carry something fragile when you don't even trust your own hands. It's having someone see you at your most undignified, and still choosing to stick by your side. It's the awful realization that you can't hate yourself into becoming a better person. You can only do one decent thing after another. And hope that it matters to someone.
