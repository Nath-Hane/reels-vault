## Transcription audio
If I had to re-learn how to handstand as a beginner, this is what I would do. I would actually start with drills off the wall rather than on the wall, building up to something like the crow pose because this is a great way to learn how to rebalance from the fingers. But also work on building up to the headstand because this is a great way to learn how to rebalance from the hips. And building up to cartwheels because this is a great way to teach you how to kick up in a way that's similar to the handstand, but also a great way to teach you how to bail out of the handstand when you lose balance to exit the handstand safely. Once I felt comfortable with all three, I would work on the forearm stand as a way to teach myself how to rebalance from the shoulder. I would also split my handstand specific practice into two parts. The kick up starting low with my hands on the ground and gradually kicking up over time. If I lose balance, I can bail out safely and that's why the cartwheel is super important. I would also practice handstands along the wall and build up to 10 to 20 seconds of free-standing practice keeping my legs split one foot near the wall and if I lose balance, I'll bail out safely. And finally putting it all together, kicking up, catching the handstand, relying on my fingers to rebalance and when things get a little too aggressive, I'm rebalancing either from the hip or the shoulders like in the headstand or the forearm stand. Of course throughout that whole time you want to be supplementing with strength and mobility training whether it's with weight or your own body weight, it doesn't matter. But developing stronger and more mobile shoulders, a stronger and more stable core, and stronger and more flexible legs is drastically going to help your handstand practice.
