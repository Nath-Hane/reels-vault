# Video by enzo.laclotte

## Description
The best cinematography trick for a beginner to master the cinematic look:

1. Camera Recording Settings: the first step of turning on your camera is going inside of the camera recording settings. Manually change the setting to 23.98 fps rather than 60 fps. This will help feel your footage to become an industry standard feeling and tone to your footage. 

2. Shutter Speed / Angle: the cinematic setting for your motion of still images to feel like a cinematic film is by changing it to 1/45 or 180°. This is a normal standard industry setting that will help your footage to have a cinematic tone of normal motion.

3. Aperture: the most cinematic look that I’ve been taught is having a T4 - T5.6 aperture to have the best combination of shallow, but slightly deep focus of your field of view that will enhance your contrast with your highlights and shadows. 

4. ND Filters: shooting 15 minutes before golden hour is enough preparation for the sunlight to have a soft tone, but also brings the highlights and the amount of luminance to be at its peak, but with an ND filter of 0.3-0.6, which is equivalent to 1 to 2 stops down can help your overexposures back down to your normal look of T4 - T5.6, therefore creating the consistent cinematic look to your footage.

5. Color Grade: the beginners guide to your cinematic look to your final look of your film or footage is by creating at least 4-5 different nodes to your Davinci Resolve. This is called a “tree branch” method to allow each node to have its own category or layer: separate Primary, Log Primaries, Log wheel, High-Dynamic Range, and Curve wheel to have the best color palette results when exporting and posting your cinematic film.
