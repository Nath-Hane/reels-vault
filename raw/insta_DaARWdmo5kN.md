## Transcription audio
Rome is falling, the senate is corrupt and only one man attempts to stand against the flow, armed with nothing but words. One of the greatest trilogies I have ever read, and it is Robert Harris's Cicero Trilogy. Harris wrote three novels following the life of Marcus Tullius Cicero, one of the greatest orators Rome has ever known. It consists of Imperium, Lustrum, and finally Dictator. Together they cover around 30 years of the most dramatic period of the ancient world, the fall of the Roman Republic. But before you get bored, these books do not read like a dry old history lesson. Instead they are one of the most gripping thrillers I have ever read. Cicero is a self made man in a world of inherited power. He had no special family name, no wealth or no military glory, just one of the greatest minds of Rome in that time. He climbed from nothing to consulship which is the highest office attainable at that time through nothing but his speeches and words. And then he spends his following years defending all that he has worked so hard to build against some of history's most famous figures like Caesar and Mark Anthony. He follows Cicero through the intricacies and conniving plots of the senate, battle conspiracies and prevent civil wars. It's Shakespearean really because Shakespeare drew from the same source materials when he wrote his play Julius Caesar. Harris does a really great job of maintaining momentum across the trilogy. You know Roman history can understandably and reasonably so feel very distant but Harris makes it feel like it was last week. The corruption, the populism, slow erosion of the democratic norms by the very people who swore to protect them. It's uncomfortably familiar and perhaps quite topical. I genuinely think if you read Imperium you will read the rest of the trilogy. It is one of the best modern works of literature I have read in a very long time and it will have you gripped.
