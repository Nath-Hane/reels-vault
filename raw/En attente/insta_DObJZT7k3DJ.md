## Description
Funny Games (1997), directed by Michael Haneke, is a disturbing psychological thriller that confronts audiences with their own relationship to violence in cinema. The story follows a family held hostage by two polite yet sadistic young men, who torment them with a series of cruel “games.” What makes the film truly unsettling is its refusal to provide comfort or escape, as it strips away the safety net viewers often expect in thrillers.

Haneke deliberately breaks the fourth wall, forcing the audience to question why they are watching and what it means to consume violence as entertainment. The performances are chilling in their calmness, making the cruelty even more unbearable. Funny Games is not an easy watch, but it is a bold and provocative film that lingers, leaving viewers disturbed, reflective, and deeply unsettled.
