Hey, Mike, how can you tell the difference between a professional photo and a more amateur looking one? Well, I hate to say it, but there's always a few easy giveaways. Firstly, you can always tell if someone's a newer photographer if they try to keep every single color in their images. A big part of photo editing is removing colors that are distracting or that don't suit the style. Newer photographers just leave everything in, and so they feel crowded and messy. The second dead giveaway is underexposed images. I don't know why, but a lot of newer photographers tend to underexpose their skin tones way too much, and then they try to bring them back up with editing, but their final images are often still underexposed. The third way, and this is a really big one, is that newer photographers try to maximize the dynamic range of every image. They just go in and remove contrast by pulling their highlights down and pushing the shadows back up. You could elevate your entire portfolio by just adding contrast back in and smoothing out the white and black points. This means you'll never have true white or true black in your images, but that's okay. Honestly, I think it's just such a classy look. And finally, color grading. If you're not color grading or adding color contrast, it's a dead giveaway that you're a new photographer. Professional photographers aren't scared to give their images a signature style by using color grading. Just relying on the ambient colors is never going to give your portfolio a consistent look and feel.
